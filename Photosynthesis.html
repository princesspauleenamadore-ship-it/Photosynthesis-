What is Photosynthesis?

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy from the Sun into chemical energy stored in glucose (sugar).

It uses carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil, and releases oxygen into the atmosphere.

It is the foundation of life because almost every living thing depends on it for food or oxygen.

Chemical Equation

Carbon Dioxide + Water + Sunlight → Glucose + Oxygen

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

How Does It Work?

Step 1: Capturing Energy

Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and traps energy inside the chloroplasts of leaf cells.

🌿 Plants appear green because they reflect green light.

Step 2: Collecting Materials

Roots absorb water from the soil while leaves take in carbon dioxide through stomata.

💧 Water is essential for plant survival.

Step 3: Transforming Energy

Light energy splits water molecules and combines them with carbon dioxide to create glucose.

Step 4: Making Food

Plants use glucose for growth and release oxygen into the atmosphere.

Why is Photosynthesis Important?

🌱 Oxygen Production

Photosynthesis produces most of the oxygen we breathe.

🥗 Food Source

Plants are the beginning of every food chain on Earth.

🌍 Climate Balance

Plants absorb carbon dioxide and help regulate climate.

🌳 Raw Materials

Provides wood, food, medicines, paper, and fuel.

Real-Life Applications

🚜 Farmers

Farmers depend on sunlight, water, and healthy plants to grow crops for food.

🌳 Forests

Forests act as the “lungs of the Earth” by producing oxygen.

🌍 Global Warming

More plants help absorb carbon dioxide and reduce warming.
